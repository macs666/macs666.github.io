I learn by building.

In college I taught myself to make things work. One of my first projects taught a camera to recognise what it saw (OpenCV, image processing). That was my first step into AI. I also built small computers (Arduino, Raspberry Pi), web servers (Node.js) and iPhone apps.

I can lead a team.

I founded Eygert Tech and led 6 engineers. We built phone apps, websites and connected devices for clients. I set the technical plan, reviewed the code and worked with the clients.

I keep going when something fails.

In 2019 the startup closed. The switch on the board opens here. I joined a software company and kept building.

I ship apps people use.

At Exalture I built the iPhone apps for Qatar Museum and for Abraj, a real estate group in Qatar.

I can build a large system from zero.

IMA360 needed a backend in Go, a language I hadn't used. I learned it in one month. Then, as the only backend engineer, I designed the whole system: it works out prices and rebates for big companies like McKesson, across millions of records. The team grew from 6 to about 30 engineers building on that design.

I build AI agents that do real work.

Since August 2026 I'm Chief AI Researcher and Engineer at Recapp (Cascade Labs). I built Recapp's own AI system: agents for sales, design, development and testing that work alongside each person on the team. I use RAG (giving AI the right company facts) and Google ADK (a toolkit for building agents). Every step before this is why the agents hold up: they run on the kind of backend I've built for 10 years.
